Models & Methods

What kind of artificial intelligence does the field actually use? At the highest level the corpus splits into deep learning, classical machine learning, radiomics (feature-based), and other. Underneath, specific architectures and algorithms differ sharply in citation impact. Papers may employ more than one approach, so counts overlap rather than partition the corpus.

Methodological Rigor & Open Science

Gradual maturation: external validation and multi-center data rose from 0% (2017) to peaks around 2021–2024. But open code remains near zero and open data is entirely absent across the decade — the field's clearest unmet standard.

External validation and multi-center adoption climbed steadily; code availability first appears only in 2025 (3.6%); open data never exceeds 0%. The 2025 code uptick signals nascent — but fragile — open-science adoption.

Notable Gaps & Recommendations

Four structural gaps emerge — and each maps to a concrete recommendation for the American Journal of Neuroradiology and the wider neuroradiology artificial intelligence community.

Open-science deficit

Open code availability remains near zero (3.6% in 2025); open data is entirely absent across the decade.

→ Prioritize open-science mandates in artificial intelligence submissions.

Subfield fragmentation

Duplicate and case-variant labels (for example, "detection / classification" appearing in several casings) distort growth analysis.

→ Standardize subfield taxonomy in future bibliometrics.

Emerging-trend blind spot

No reliable data on emerging keywords, methods, or benchmarks could be extracted from the corpus.

→ Track keyword bursts and benchmark adoption longitudinally.

Geographic concentration

United States institutions dominate (14 of 15 top institutions); no non-United-States leaders appear in the rankings.

→ Encourage global collaboration in artificial intelligence neuroradiology research.
