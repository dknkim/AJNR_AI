

# Bibliometric Analysis of AI Research in the *American Journal of Neuroradiology* (2017–2026)

## 1. Executive Summary

AI research in the *American Journal of Neuroradiology* (AJNR) has evolved from a nascent field into a dominant research area over the past decade, characterized by exponential growth in publication volume and significant citation impact. The corpus comprises **229 papers** published between **2017 and 2026**, accumulating **5,456 total citations** with an **age-adjusted total of 912.0 citations** (accounting for publication year). This represents a mean citation rate of 23.83 per paper, indicating strong scholarly resonance despite the field's relative youth.

The trajectory reveals a clear acceleration in AI research output, with publication volume surging from 6 papers in 2017 to a peak of **55 papers in 2025**. Citation impact, however, peaked in **2021** (235.7 age-adjusted citations), reflecting a period of high influence despite lower volume than 2025. This suggests a maturation phase where impactful foundational work (e.g., clinical translation, multimodal integration) gained traction in 2021, followed by a volume-driven expansion in 2025. The field has transitioned from early exploratory studies (2017–2018) to clinically focused applications (2021–2026), with AI moving from *proof-of-concept* to *clinical decision support*.

Dominant subfields are **Detection / classification (CADx)** (158 papers), **Quantitative imaging** (149 papers), and **Image segmentation** (132 papers), collectively representing 80% of the corpus. The fastest-growing subfield is **Prognosis / outcome prediction** (CAGR 20.85%), driven by AI’s role in personalized treatment planning. Declining subfields include *Machine learning (general)*, *Machine learning*, and *multimodal AI* (all declining from 1 to 0 papers in the recent period), though the main *Multimodal AI* subfield (12 papers) shows growth.

The most influential work centers on **high-impact clinical translation**. The single most-cited paper—*Deep-learning convolutional neural networks accurately classify genetic mutations in gliomas* (2018, 437 citations)—exemplifies this trend, integrating *genetic classification*, *explainability*, and *multimodal AI*. Emerging methods like **radiomics** (mean citations: 57.6) and **convolutional neural networks** (mean citations: 115.3) dominate high-impact work, while benchmarks like *The Cancer Imaging Archives* (mean citations: 437.0) underpin reproducibility.

Institutional leadership is split between high-volume contributors (e.g., *Stanford University School of Medicine*, 23 papers) and high-impact centers (e.g., *Harvard Medical School*, mean citations: 23.9). Author leadership is led by **Zaharchuk G.** (18 papers, 1,085 citations) and **Filippi C.G.** (6 papers, 968 citations, highest mean: 161.3), reflecting a pattern where a few authors drive outsized influence through concentrated, high-impact work.

## 10. Notable Gaps and Recommendations

1. **Methodological gaps**: Open code availability remains near-zero (3.6% in 2025), and open data is absent. *Recommendation: Prioritize open-science mandates in AJNR AI submissions.*
2. **Subfield fragmentation**: Duplicate subfield labels (e.g., "detection / classification (CADx)" vs. "Detection / classification (CADx)") complicate analysis. *Recommendation: Standardize subfield taxonomy in future bibliometrics.*
3. **Emerging trends**: No reliable data on emerging keywords or benchmarks. *Recommendation: Track keyword bursts and benchmark adoption in longitudinal studies.*
4. **Institutional bias**: U.S. institutions dominate (14/15 top institutions), with no non-U.S. leaders. *Recommendation: Encourage global collaboration in AI neuroradiology research.*
