# Executive Summary

Over the past decade, the American Journal of Neuroradiology (AJNR) has published 250 AI-related papers, spanning from 2017 to 2026. These papers have collectively garnered 5,627 citations, with an age-adjusted total of 941 citations, reflecting a robust and growing interest in AI applications within neuroradiology. The mean citations per paper stand at 22.51, indicating a significant impact of this research.

### Trend Trajectory of AI Publishing
The publication output in AI-related research has shown a notable acceleration over the years. The peak in both volume and citation impact occurred in 2021, with 33 papers and 1,438 citations. However, the age-adjusted citation impact was highest in 2021 as well, with 239.7 citations. This suggests that 2021 was a pivotal year for both quantity and quality of AI research in neuroradiology. The trend indicates a steady increase in the number of publications, with a slight dip in 2026, possibly due to the early stage of the year.

### Dominant Subfields and Growth Trends
The dominant subfields in AI research include "Detection / classification (CADx)" with 186 papers, "Quantitative imaging" with 126 papers, and "Image segmentation" with 102 papers. The fastest-growing subfields are "Explainability / trustworthy AI" and "Image reconstruction / acceleration," both showing a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 24.14% and 23%, respectively. Conversely, "Prognosis / outcome prediction" and "Quantitative imaging" have seen slower growth, with CAGRs of 22.03% and 9.87%, respectively.

### Emerging Methods, Keywords, and Benchmarks
Emerging methods and keywords in the field include deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and radiomics. These methods are increasingly being applied to tasks such as image segmentation, detection, and prognosis. Notably, large language models (LLMs) and federated learning are gaining traction, particularly in recent years.

### Most-Cited Paper
The single most-cited paper is "Deep-learning convolutional neural networks accurately classify genetic mutations in gliomas" (2018), with 437 citations and a citations-per-year rate of 48.6. This paper has significantly influenced the field by demonstrating the potential of deep learning in genetic mutation classification.

### Influential Authors and Institutions
The most influential authors in the field are Zaharchuk G. (18 papers, 1085 citations), Filippi C.G. (6 papers, 968 citations), and Wintermark M. (12 papers, 929 citations). Stanford University School of Medicine and Stanford University lead the institutional rankings with 23 and 15 papers, respectively, and 1382 and 1114 citations, respectively.

## Emerging Keywords, Methods, and Benchmarks

### Emerging Methods
- **Deep Learning**: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)
- **Radiomics**: Quantitative feature extraction and analysis
- **Large Language Models (LLMs)**: Integration of natural language processing (NLP) in medical imaging
- **Federated Learning**: Privacy-preserving collaborative learning

### Emerging Benchmarks
- **Image Segmentation**: Dice coefficient, Jaccard index
- **Classification**: Accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score
- **Prognosis**: Area Under the Curve (AUC), Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve
